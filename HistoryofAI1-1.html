Origins of artificial intelligence

Success criteria: Identify early ideas, key events, and the origin of the term.

Can machines think?

In 1950, Alan Turing posed a fundamental question: “Can machines think?” He devised a test for machine intelligence later known as the Turing Test. In this test, a human judge has natural language conversations with both a human and a machine without knowing which is which. If the judge cannot reliably tell the machine from the human, the machine passes.

Naming the field

In 1956, researchers at the Dartmouth Workshop started a project on machine intelligence and made the term “artificial intelligence” official. This milestone marked AI as a distinct academic discipline rather than just a theoretical question.

Rules and logic

During the 1960s and 1970s, AI research focused on rule-based logic and structured human knowledge. Researchers believed that providing enough rules and facts would allow machines to reason like humans. However, relying strictly on symbolic logic made it difficult for systems to grasp context, the nuance of natural language, perception, or self-correction.

What was the main limitation of AI research during the 1960s–1970s?
