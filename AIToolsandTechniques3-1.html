Module 03: AI tools and techniques, lesson 1 of 3

Prompt engineering

Success criteria: Write clear, effective prompts and recognize common prompting strategies.

Prompt engineering is designing a prompt to guide an AI model toward a more accurate answer. Using the techniques below together helps AI give a better response.

How to write a good prompt

Formatting
The format of a prompt affects how the AI interprets it, and different models may work better with different formats: natural-language instructions, bullet points, or structured formats like JSON. Knowing what your model prefers helps you write good prompts.
Context and examples
Giving context and examples helps the AI understand the task. If you want a story, describing the theme you're aiming for will help it write a better one.
Instructions and constraints
Clearly explain what you want and avoid being vague. Add constraints to keep the AI on track, such as a character limit, a tone, topics to avoid, or things the response must include.
Defining the output
Tell the AI how to present its answer: a table, chart, bullet points, numbered list, short summary, step-by-step explanation, JSON or a detailed response.
Fine-tuning and adapting
Fine-tuning trains an existing model on a smaller, task-specific dataset to improve it at a certain task. Adapting means giving the AI feedback it can build on to improve its next response.
Testing and refining
Prompting is usually a repeat process. Your first prompt probably won't give the result you want, so evaluate the response, find what's missing, and adjust your instructions using the methods above.

Prompting strategies

Zero-shot prompting
Giving the AI a task with no examples, so it relies on what it already knows. Results tend to be broad. Example: “Explain how to do long division.”
Few-shot prompting
Giving the AI a few examples of the response you want. For instance, show it several product descriptions, then ask it to write another one in the same style.
Role prompting
Assigning the AI a role or perspective to shape its answer, like asking it to act as a physicist explaining gravity to a beginner. The skits online of AI talking in internet slang are role prompting too.
Chain-of-thought prompting
Encouraging the AI to work through a problem step by step instead of jumping to an answer. It helps with complex, multi-step tasks, like a math problem. Newer models often do this on their own.
Iterative prompting
Improving your prompt by using the AI's previous response to see what to change. For example, ask for an essay about global warming, then ask again but make it more emotionally persuasive.
