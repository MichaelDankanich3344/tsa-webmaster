Training AI with language

Success criteria: Understand how language models are trained and where NLP is used.

An AI model doesn't know a language on its own. Instead, it's trained on huge collections of text as well as individual examples. Training lets the model learn the typical relationships between words, sentences and concepts.

Training is how people teach an AI model to spot patterns. The model is shown examples of data, then changed based on how well it did. This repeats until the model becomes good at recognizing patterns. The training data includes:

  • Books and articles
  • Websites
  • Educational materials such as textbooks

Next-token prediction is a common training method. The model learns to predict which token (a word or piece of a word) should come next based on the text before it. Doing this over and over helps the system learn the patterns of language.

The training loop

These three steps repeat millions of times.

  1. Prediction. The model predicts the next token.
  2. Evaluation. The training system measures how wrong the prediction was.
  3. Adjustment. The model's internal weights are adjusted to improve future predictions.

Two phases of training

Pre-training
The model learns general language patterns from large amounts of text, including grammar, vocabulary and relationships between words.
Post-training
The model is trained further to follow instructions and give more useful responses. Two common techniques:
  • Supervised fine-tuning (SFT): learning from examples of instructions and good responses.
  • Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF): using people's ratings to encourage more helpful and appropriate responses.

Real-world applications

NLP is used in everyday tools such as ChatGPT, Siri and Alexa to answer questions, and in customer service chatbots. Businesses also use it to sort through thousands of reviews and to filter spam email.
