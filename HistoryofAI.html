Origins of the field

When did AI become a field of study? Sucess Criteria: Identify early ideas, events, and the origin of the term.

In 1950, Alan Turing proposes a question, “Can machines think” this test for machine intelligence which would be later known as the “Turing Test”. In the test, a human judges in conversations with both a human and a machine without being provided with the knowledge with who is who. If the person guesses the machine was human, then the machine passes the test, although no computer has passed. In 1956 a group of scientists at the University of Darthmouth Workshop they began a research project in machine intelligence would call it, “artificial intelligence” this is where it was marked as a field of study to research, rather than it just remaining as a question to be experimented on. In the 1960-1970s AI research was focused on applying human comprehension to a machine which included logic, rules, and knowledge that was structured. Researchers believed that if enough information was provided such as rules or facts the machine can think and reason like a human would. The problem was there were a lot of issues with just relying on logic, such as having a machine to understand language, context, perception, or when it was wrong.
